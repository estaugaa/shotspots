# Decisions (newest first)

One line each: what was decided, and why. Add a line when a choice is made; edit a line when it is reversed.

- 2026-11-23 Time zones for photo timing: sunlite.tz_for() knew 10 western states and gave every other spot Pacific time, so Commons/photostats capture times east of the Rockies were read 1-3 h late (median share at sunrise 2%, at sunset 24%, among 2,079 spots with 10+ photographers; Cadillac Mountain 'few photographers shoot here at sunrise'). Now the time zone of the nearest US airport station (Iowa Mesonet tzname, data/photoweather/stations.json), which also gets split states right (Pensacola, Gary IN, Chattanooga, Ontario OR checked), with a 51-entry state table as fallback. spotphotos.json carries per-event baselines and is rebuilt daily. Not shipped: lift counted only in urban areas (it promoted an urban garden to first place; needs truth data, roadmap 5.1).
- 2026-11-23 More webcam truth. ALERTCalifornia's 1,310 ridge-top cameras (UC San Diego; CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, credit 'ALERTCalifornia | UC San Diego'; non-commercial use with credit, no derivatives) publish each camera's live heading and tilt, so only frames looking within 50 deg of the sun and near the horizon are taken (267 cameras faced the sun one Nov 23 afternoon); only numbers are kept from them, no thumbnails (no derivatives; the data branch is public). The probe point toward the sun uses the real sun azimuth (sunlite.sun_az; was a fixed 120/240 deg). Every road-camera frame is thumbnailed (200 px, kept 14 days, was ~1 in 20) and imagerate.py rates each once and compares the formula with its horizon-gap term against 'vivid' on the same frames. camtruth.json adds agreement by camera type and for sun-facing frames. Photo weather v2: every day with a golden-hour photo (7 in 8 rated photos had no weather), areas ranked by golden-hour photographers.
- 2026-11-23 Spot quality from photographers, the same for every spot (pipeline/spotphotos.py, app photoQ). Distinct Commons photographers within 500 m (900 m peaks) from the full 3.08 M-photo history; ~30 = fully famous; city spots count fully only if they stand out from the 5 km around them (lift >= 15x), because Commons is heavy with city, event and transit photos (Times Square 493, a convention centre 302). Before: hand spots used Commons geotag counts only, starter spots Wikipedia/OSM, on different scales (Haystack Rock = an unknown waterfall). Spots absent from the feed (nobody photographs the area) are unknown: median of measured spots nearby.
- 2026-11-23 New layer "When photographers shoot here" (app/phototime.js): share of a spot's photographers out at that golden hour vs the ~12% norm; +15% / -20% at most, weighted by evidence (5 photographers = half). The old photostats geosearch capped at 100 photos (Multnomah: 8 dated).
- 2026-11-21 First validation runs. Golden-hour photo share (24 cells, 8,542 events): within +-2 points in every cloud band, formula agreement 0.005; visitors' schedules decide it, so it is not a tuning target. Image rating (2,999 Commons photos, 1,119 with weather): rain and solid low cloud cut 'vivid' as expected, but formula agreement 0.05 because most golden-hour photos are of buildings and people. Webcams: warm-pixel share agrees with the image model (vivid 0.54, aesthetic 0.46), so that colour measure is sound; its limit is volume. Changes: the rater now keeps the sky-prompt scores, rates files named sunset/sunrise first, and analyses sky photos only (half or more of the model's answer a sky/weather prompt, or named so), with vivid as a share of the sky answers; photo weather is fetched for cells with 10+ busy days (was 30).
- 2026-11-20 Automatic image rating instead of hand ratings (Eric): CLIP ViT-B/32 (OpenAI weights, open_clip) scores each image against 'vivid colourful sunset/sunrise sky' and other prompts, and LAION's aesthetic predictor v1 (MIT linear head, trained on people's ratings) gives a 1-10 look score. pipeline/imagerate.py rates ~3,000 Commons golden-hour photos a day in cells with ERA5 weather (thumbnails 330 px, only scores kept) and the saved webcam thumbnails, and reports which measure tracks the look: the sky formula vs vivid, and the webcams' warm-pixel share vs vivid. It answers the photo-share caveat (people out vs sky lit up). torch/open_clip install only for this job (requirements-ml.txt). The extraction keeps file page ids (v3) so images can be fetched.
- 2026-11-20 Terrain in the golden-hour score, from data (Commons photo history: 287 spots with a 10 m horizon profile and >= 20 photo days; share of photo days at sunset, bootstrap 95% ranges): terrain 5 deg or more toward the sun x0.75 (0.58-0.93) vs under 2 deg, and differences under 2 deg none; 'blocked' x0.74 (0.60-0.90); facing the other way x0.90 (0.80-1.02). Now: spots with a profile use the terrain height at the sun's actual direction (1.0 up to 2 deg, x0.75 from 6 deg, TUNE.blocked); others keep the 'blocked' category at x0.75; facing away x0.9 (was x0.55, TUNE.faceAway). The +4 for an open view stays: the groups compared both had it, so the test can't judge it (removing it put Ribbon Falls, in the canyon, above the Grand Canyon rim points). Top 3 unchanged in all 6 scoring-test cases; Mount Tabor at sunrise 20->36, Gorge falls at sunset 22->16, Yosemite valley falls 44->40. Limit: popular spots only, and photo days measure where people go, not colour.
- 2026-11-20 Validation history from Wikimedia Commons dumps, not Flickr's YFCC100M: the webcam truth covers 3 days and its colour measure scored the formula's top band lowest, too thin and noisy to tune on. YFCC100M's file is readable in a public AWS bucket, but its metadata is under Yahoo's Webscope licence, which must be agreed before access and isn't being granted now (only ICSI's derived features are CC0), so it is not used. Commons dumps are openly licensed; pipeline/commonsphotos.py streams three tables (~25 GB: geotags, pages, image EXIF) on Actions and keeps place, capture time, brightness (EV) and name words for every US-geotagged photo with a time (sample: 17% of files carry a capture time). Next step joins these with historical weather: how often people photographed a sunrise/sunset, against the conditions. The Commons API is not used for bulk (HTTP 429 within a few requests). First run (Oct 2026 dump, 90 min): 3,083,807 US photos with a capture time of 4,029,348 US-geotagged files. The measure is photographers, not photos (one uploader's 200 frames of one sunset are one vote; v2 of the file keeps the uploader number). pipeline/photoweather.py: share of a cell-day's photographers (0.25 deg, >= 3 out) who shot the golden hour, relative to that cell's usual share for the event and season, against ERA5 cloud at the sunrise/sunset hour 2016-2025 (Open-Meteo archive, ~35 cells a day within the free limit, busiest first); the sky formula's Spearman and best weights. It measures 'worth going out for', which includes pleasant weather, and has no horizon-gap term yet. Time zones from the nearest airport station (Iowa Mesonet lists carry tzname), so no new dependency.
- 2026-11-21 Webcam truth, 3-day review (3,667 frames): weights NOT changed. Relative Spearman 0.06 and colour fell at the highest scores, because the warm-sky measure was counting an LED road sign, tail and street lights, brown hillsides and a dead-feed placeholder; only 2 of 10 sampled frames were real sunset colour. Measure v2: warm = lit sky glow (sat 0.18-0.62, value >= 0.4, orange or pink, smooth); a frame is used only if its sky band is >= 25 levels brighter than the road and not featureless (fog, glare); placeholders and repeated frames (frozen feeds) dropped. Kept 100 of 275 thumbnails; the top-rated are real glows. v1 rows are ignored, so the 3-day clock restarts (review Nov 25).
- 2026-11-19 Fog in scoring: forecast visibility under 3 km in a morning window, scored only up to 18 h ahead (FOG_LEAD_H, app.js); later fog is mentioned ("fog possible"), and neither the "above the fog" viewpoint boosts nor the fog-tops boost apply. Tested against airport reports at sunrise (tools/fogrule.py, 12 days, 75 stations): same day the old rule caught 67% of fog with 51% of calls right, and its "humid and calm" half added mostly false alarms; visibility alone caught the same 67% with 60% right. A day ahead no rule had skill (15% caught, 26% right). 18 h is a placeholder until the evening snapshot (verify_obs.snapshot_evening, ~03 UTC daily) measures the ~12 h lead.
- 2026-11-18 Load speed, from a phone-like load profile (tools/loadprofile.py, slow 4G + CPU x4, after every publish): the cost was script time, not downloads (373 KB cold, 0 KB warm). Ranking every spot for every window of the week took 20-35 s of the first 30 and replaced the top cards 2-3 times. Now: exact-result memos (distances, local dates, date formatters, airport matches); the visible day ranked first, the rest of the week 3 s after data settles; each spot scored once per redraw (app/perf.js); placeholders until forecast and nearby tiles are in (max 3.5 s), then the top picks keep their order and a floating pill offers a re-sort; the service worker serves the app's files cache-first from the build-versioned cache and falls back to cached feeds after 3 s. The profile then watched only the picks list (not every card on the page) and showed the first list could still swap a pick when real road times replaced the estimate, and be pushed down by late lines above it. So the first list also waits for core data and its top 10 candidates' road times (same 3.5 s cap), the "right now" line holds its space while loading, the headline and its reasons keep two lines' height on phones, and the "headline is for here" note sits under the picks. Late data (HRRR, calibration, airport reports, fame) now redraws 0.3 s after it lands instead of waiting for the next redraw: whether a phone showed it depended on whether anything else redrew later (part of the Mt St Helens flake). The rest of it: the summit-cloud forecast was fetched 2.5 s after start for the peaks known then and kept an hour, so peaks from spot tiles that loaded later (Sawtooth Mountain on a slow run) had no forecast; it is now fetched again when the nearby peaks change. Road-time requests share one store per place (two in flight used to overwrite each other). Nov 19, the last two movements (profile now names which card grew and what each layout shift moved): the airport-report reason arrived after the picks and added a line to every card, and the alert line was inserted above everything when the alerts feed landed (54 px down, ~1 s in). The first list now also waits for airport reports, calibration, HRRR where it is used, and the eager feeds (same 3.5 s cap); the alert line appears with the list, its slot kept blank meanwhile if the last visit had one. Nov 19, script time (profile by caller): sunrise/sunset/moon times shared their step positions across the threshold scans, park lookups and species lists are remembered per spot, and sightings get a latitude check before the distance (same results; these functions 3.1 s -> 1.8 s per return visit on a desktop, ~4x that on the phone profile). Then sun and moon times per place and day (~5,000, ~3 s on a desktop) are kept between visits (localStorage ss2:dayev, today onward, exact): a return visit at CPU x4 ran 14-20 s of script in 25 s before, ~6 s after (5 of 6 runs). The slow-4G profile showed the 54 px push was the Light / Night sky / Calendar switch, added by a later script after an earlier one had drawn the page: nothing is drawn until every script has run (DOMContentLoaded). The "right now" line stays (saying the weather is unavailable) when its request fails, and the alert slot is also kept once the alerts are in. Nov 19, the 3 s return-visit list: not the service worker's 3 s feed fallback (no feed waited for it). The list waits for the eager feeds (light 1.2 MB and hrrr 0.5 MB parse by ~1.9 s at CPU x4) plus one full scoring pass (~0.9 s); both are needed for scores that don't change after showing. Spacing out redraws stays rejected (see above). Fixed the waste found on the way: the spot tiles were loaded twice per visit (loadSeed started at 400 ms and again from loadNearby before the first had finished), so 6 folders x 12 tiles were fetched and parsed twice; a second call now waits for the first. The live profile after it showed no list-time change (3.3 s, within run-to-run spread); the cost left is scoring: nearest forecast point lookups for probe points (90 km toward the sun, 25 km each way for rainbows) differ every window, so they miss the memo. The scan now keeps each point's sin/cos of latitude and takes the arccos only where a point can be nearest (same operations, same answers: 0 differences in 393k lookups, 7.7x faster), and the peaks near a spot are remembered per spot. Return visit after: picks 1.8 s, script 3.2 s in 30 s. The same profile's first visit had the forecast at 7.1 s: the 3.5 s cap showed the list without it ("forecast loading" on every card) and pinned those picks; with the forecast held back 6 s the pinned picks scored 44-48 while the real best scored 78-90. The 3.5 s cap now starts only once the forecast is in; with none the list waits up to 12 s.
- 2026-11-18 Not done: spacing out data-driven redraws. Drive times and iNaturalist sightings are fetched for the spots a redraw shows, so fewer redraws left them unfetched and changed the list (the scoring test caught it).
- 2026-11-18 Forecast caches key on time plus version (wxVer) and late answers are dropped (loadWx, HRRR): a replaced forecast with the same timestamp kept old nearest points; the scoring baseline was re-recorded (8 far spots corrected, top 10s unchanged).
- 2026-11-18 Trails (up to ~5 h a run) has its own queue: in the shared pipeline queue it held up every 30-minute GOES update while it ran (each waiting run was replaced and cancelled). Concurrent writers are safe since the data branch save re-applies each job's files.
- 2026-11-18 Forecast check compares like with like (verify_obs method 2): Open-Meteo low cloud (up to ~6,500 ft) vs every reported layer up to 6,500 ft (IEM), fog only where the model gave visibility. Method 1 compared low cloud with a 1,500 ft ceiling and used low cloud as a fog stand-in, which inflated the false alarms and the +21.6 bias; no scoring change is made on method-1 figures, and the app shows fog/low-cloud accuracy only from method 2.
- 2026-11-18 Workflow pushes fail loudly after 3 tries (the old loops ended on `sleep`, so a lost push reported success). Runners pinned to ubuntu-24.04 (ubuntu-latest moves to 26.04, where Playwright can't install); actions on their Node 24 majors (checkout v7, setup-python v7, cache v6, Pages v5).
- 2026-11-17 Data depth shown, not hidden (app/depth.js, display only): four dots on every card (terrain, facing, photo record, routed drive; filled = measured) and a "Data behind it" line under "Why this score". Where fog or low cloud is a main reason, a note gives the measured day-ahead record (verify_obs: nearest verified airport with >=20 reports, else the whole network, nothing beyond 250 km). The figure shown is "came true when forecast" (1 - false-alarm ratio), since that is what a fog-boosted score relies on; the hit rate is on the spot page too. The feed refresh drops verify_obs without refetching it, so depth.js and the Settings accuracy box (prefs.js) fetch it again when missing.
- 2026-11-17 Observations are kept indefinitely in monthly gzip CSV archives (data/archive/<kind>/<YYYY-MM>.csv.gz, one row per report; pipeline/archive.py): airport reports, NWS fog advisories, satellite scans, webcam truth, forecast-check pairs. Rolling 30-120 day files made seasonal and year-on-year checks impossible. Readers take their window from the recent months (verify_obs 60 d, skill 60 d, camtruth fit 30 d). Old files are moved in on each job's first run, then removed.
- 2026-11-17 Prediction log: each night the live site ranks the next sunrise and sunset for 8 fixed places (tools/scoretest.py --live) and the top 20 with every layer's score go to data/archive/predictions; tools/predeval.py joins them with webcam and airport truth (Spearman). Needed before the v2 weights can be tuned against outcomes.
- 2026-11-17 Seasonal sky odds come from a national sunrise/sunset climatology (pipeline/climonat.py, job=climonat): every US ASOS/AWOS station with 5+ years in the free IEM archive, 10 years, the report nearest each sunrise/sunset (within 45 min), counted by week of year. The old 7-station Gorge table (by month and hour) stays only as the fallback away from sunrise/sunset. Counts are stored (not percentages) so the app can show sample sizes; the app pools the week with its neighbours (~200 days). ASOS can't see cloud above 12,000 ft, which the app says.
- 2026-11-17 Only checked app code is published: data jobs republish every ~30 min and used to take app/ from main, so a change the scoring test had blocked still went live. Passing checks move branch app-verified; publish.yml takes app/ from it.
- 2026-11-17 Scoring test answers drive times and forecasts per point, not by exact URL: both are requested in batches that depend on which spot tiles have loaded, which made far spots' scores vary between identical runs.
- 2026-11-17 feed/ and data/ moved to a `data` branch, squashed daily (Eric): 647 of 956 commits on main were bot feed updates and every clone kept growing. Jobs commit only their own changed files and re-apply them if another job pushed first. Main's old history is left as it is (no rewrite).
- 2026-11-17 Data jobs install exact versions from requirements.lock (an upstream release can't break a scheduled run); tests.yml also tests the newest allowed versions and publishes their freeze to branch diag-freeze for upgrading.
- 2026-11-17 Recovered errors are counted, not silent: pipeline soft_fail() -> "soft" in feed/status/<module>.json; app noteErr() -> problem reports. Links from data go through safeUrl() (no javascript:/data: links).
- 2026-11-17 Forms rate limit fixed: the 60-per-hour check compared ISO times with SQLite's space format and was really ~60 per UTC day.
- 2026-11-17 Scoring test on frozen data (tools/scoretest.py) blocks publishing when a ranking changes: the live checks only ran nightly on changing weather, so a regression was seen after it shipped. Fixtures are recorded from the live site and replayed against the working copy's app code.
- 2026-11-19 Access rules can cover an area ([south, west, north, east]) as well as listed spots: eBird hotspots and map spots have generated ids, so a seasonal road closure has to be by place. First one: Teton Park Road (Nov 1 to Apr 30), after Jenny Lake led Jackson's wildlife list with the road shut.
- 2026-11-19 'Current weather unavailable' while the forecast loaded: the now line fetched Open-Meteo directly with no fallback and retried after 10 min. It now uses omFetch (forwarder fallback), rejects readings over 45 min old (the forwarder's stale copy), and retries after about a minute.
- 2026-11-18 Rainbows (Eric saw a double rainbow at Round Lake): the model had 0 min of sunshine all afternoon and 99% cloud, so the old rules never fired. Now: sun breaks from minutes of sunshine (not average cloud), shower weather codes count, sun up to 42 deg; the near-you check allows sun and shower within +-30 min; and a live 'rainbow watch' from airport reports (a station within 40 km reporting rain/showers + a nearby station with breaks + low sun) for the next 45 min.
- 2026-11-18 First webcam night (795 sunset frames, Oregon + California): sky formula vs real colour Spearman 0.07, best weights 0.13; one evening and a noisy measure, so defaults unchanged. Clear evenings showed more warm light on the land (3.0 vs 1.8). Added thumbnails for checking by eye and per-camera relative colour.
- 2026-11-17 Daytime windows scaled by sun height (below 12° 0.95, 12-25° 0.82, 25-40° 0.72, above 40° 0.62) instead of clock time: winter afternoons have low raking light all day.
- 2026-11-17 Webcam truth (pipeline/camtruth.py, after every satellite run): public road cameras (Oregon, California) photographed at every sunrise/sunset; real sky/land colour vs the sky formula with the forecast of the moment; Spearman and a weight search in feed/camtruth.json. Hundreds of checks a day instead of waiting for outing logs.
- 2026-11-17 Clear golden hours credit warm light on the land (64) and alpenglow (+8): a clear evening was scoring 23-32 because only cloud colour counted. Broken low cloud x0.85 (was 0.7), solid deck x0.4 (was 0.3). All weights in TUNE; #tune opens a testing panel. To be checked against webcam truth.
- 2026-11-17 Every scoring adjustment applies to every spot by what it is (kindOf: map kind, or categories for hand-researched spots); the +3 hand-research bonus is gone. (Eric)
- 2026-11-17 Daily jobs are started by tools/scheduler.py after every GOES run (~30 min), not GitHub cron alone: cron dropped the nightly check and ran job.yml's schedules hours late.
- 2026-11-17 Outside the Wildlife filter wildlife-led spots get x0.85 overall (their sunset sky still counts).
- 2026-11-17 Speed: wildlife-layer facts once per spot per redraw, nearest forecast point memoised (full redraw 7.7 s -> 2.1 s). Forecast requests race Open-Meteo against the forwarder after 2 s. Header no longer wraps while loading.
- 2026-11-17 Forecast check stores its own day-ahead forecasts (Open-Meteo's previous-runs archive has no low/mid cloud); first comparisons Nov 18.
- 2026-11-17 Forecast check (pipeline/skill.py, daily 12:20 PM PT): day-ahead forecast low cloud vs airport reports at every US sunrise/sunset; calibration applied only where a held-out test shows it lowers the error. HRRR replaces the default forecast for the next 18 h only where it measured more accurate. Data decides, not my judgment.
- 2026-11-17 Unknown is not bad: a spot with no photo/Wikipedia record gets the median quality of recorded spots nearby. Settings has "Lesser-known" (fame lowers the score instead).
- 2026-11-17 Mapillary not used for quality: its image counts measure dashcam road coverage, not how photogenic a spot is.
- 2026-11-17 National research (pipeline/deepnat.py): top 15 starter spots per 1-degree tile, most-photographed tiles first, cards in feed/deep/<tile>.json; the app computes terrain sun times from the stored horizon profile (app/terrainsun.js).
- 2026-11-17 Forecast forwarder keeps a 6-hour copy and serves it when Open-Meteo refuses or fails.
- 2026-11-17 Late data (airport reports, HRRR, calibration) applies on the next redraw rather than forcing one, so the list doesn't shift while loading.
- 2026-11-16 Nowcast: for windows in the next 3 hours, nearby airport cloud reports replace the forecast's low/mid cloud (full weight now, 30% at +3 h). The forecast said 100% overcast at Washougal while airports and Eric saw partly cloudy.
- 2026-11-16 Spot quality outside Wildlife counts photos only (Commons, Flickr); iNaturalist sightings measure naturalist activity, not views.
- 2026-11-16 Scoring is data driven, not coverage driven: hand-researched spots get quality from the same measures as every spot (photo and sighting counts, Wikipedia, photographer stands), not a fixed bonus. Outside Wildlife, any spot that is mainly about animals competes on its view. (Eric)
- 2026-11-16 Starter-spot horizons use the highest ground within 30 m and ignore terrain closer than 40 m: rough pins below a rim saw a false 25-degree wall.
